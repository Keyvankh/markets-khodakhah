Daily analysis · Wednesday 25 November 2026

Gold, Bitcoin and the Nasdaq ended the day in the bottom of their ranges

Hot US inflation and a near-certain December Fed hike set off a sharp sell-off. A late recovery cut the Nasdaq 100's fall from 4.4% to 0.6%, but gold, Bitcoin and the Nasdaq 100 fund still sit in the discount zone of their recent range.

Today in brief

  • US inflation came in hot and growth was revised up. October core PCE rose to 3.3% year on year (forecast 3.2%), headline PCE to 3.8%, Q3 GDP was revised to 4.3% annualised (forecast 3.5%) and jobless claims fell to 189,000. CME FedWatch puts the chance of a Fed hike on 9 December at about 94-98%.
  • A sharp sell-off turned into a strong recovery. The Nasdaq 100 was down more than 4% in the US morning but closed only 0.64% lower at 26,046.90. The S&P 500 slipped 0.25% to 7,182.78, the Dow rose 0.33% back above 50,000 (50,182.95) and most S&P 500 stocks ended higher.
  • AI stocks stayed under pressure. Nvidia closed 3.91% lower, down 27% in November, and S&P Global put its AA credit rating on CreditWatch Negative. Meta bucked the trend with a 3.4% gain.
  • New US tariff threat. The US Trade Representative proposed Section 301 tariffs of 15-20% on China, the EU, Japan and other partners from January at the earliest; the US-China truce expires on 10 January.
  • Gold recovered from its low; silver hit a 14-month low. Gold fell as far as $3,426.40 and closed at $3,534.70, down 0.71%. Silver touched $39.34, its lowest in 14 months.
  • Crypto steadied at low levels. Bitcoin was near $60,100 in the US evening after trading near $57,900, with US spot bitcoin funds heading for their worst month (about $5 billion of outflows in November).
  • Holiday trading ahead. US markets are closed on Thursday for Thanksgiving and close early on Friday, so liquidity will be thin.

United States

Data (October unless noted; actual / forecast / previous):

  • PCE prices: +0.3% m/m / +0.3% / +0.5%; 3.8% y/y / 3.8% / 3.6% (the highest since 2023).
  • Core PCE: +0.3% m/m / +0.3% / +0.3%; 3.3% y/y / 3.2% / 3.1% (the highest since late 2023).
  • Q3 GDP, second estimate: 4.3% annualised / 3.5% / advance 3.4%. Private domestic final sales grew 5.8%; corporate profits rose $206bn, against $384bn in Q2.
  • Personal income: +0.7% / about +0.3-0.4% / +0.2%. Spending: +0.3% / +0.4% / +0.5%. The saving rate rose to 4.1%.
  • Initial jobless claims (week to 21 November): 189,000 / 200,000 / 203,000. Continuing claims fell to about 1.70 million.
  • Durable goods orders: +0.2% / forecasts ranged from +0.5% to +1.1% / +1.9%.

On Tuesday, Conference Board consumer confidence jumped to 103.3 from 91.9 (forecast about 92.5).

The Fed. The funds rate is 3.75-4.00%. Two members wanted a hike at the October meeting. After today's figures, CME FedWatch puts the chance of a 25 bp hike on 9 December at about 94-98%, and Bitcoin.com reports roughly 63% for another hike in January. Some of the debate has moved on to whether December could bring 50 bp. Goldman Sachs expects a December hike and possibly one in January; Deutsche Bank expects hikes in December, January and March, to 4.75%. The Fed is in its pre-meeting blackout.

Markets. Stocks opened sharply lower (Nasdaq Composite -4.5% at the open) and recovered part of the loss by midday. Three forces drove the fall. First, hot core inflation strengthened the case for tighter policy. Second, the tariff proposal raised fresh trade-war risk. Third, confidence in the AI trade kept weakening. Nvidia fell about 8% even though Jensen Huang returned as chief executive on Monday. Investors are worried about profitability, overinvestment and an independent board investigation, and Huang is due before Congress next week. Lam Research and Micron fell more than 10%; credit stress showed in wider credit default swap spreads at SoftBank and CoreWeave. Treasury yields fell, with the 10-year at about 4.82%, as investors sought safety. The dollar index held above 104. HP rose about 11% after its results; Deere fell about 3% after an earnings miss.

United Kingdom

There was no major UK data today. Bank Rate is 4.00% after the Bank of England's 25 bp hike on 4 November (8-1 vote; one member wanted 4.25%). Markets price more than an 80% chance of another 25 bp hike on 17 December. UK CPI was 3.5% in September, and the Bank expects inflation near 5% by mid-2027. Sterling held just above $1.31, near its weakest level against the dollar since April 2025. The FTSE 100 closed about 0.6% lower after giving up early gains as the US tech sell-off spread. Gilt yields fell with Treasuries, but I could not confirm a level. The Autumn Budget (28 October) is behind us, with the OBR forecasting inflation of 3.4% in 2027 and fiscal headroom of about £15bn.

Europe and the world

  • Euro area. The ECB deposit rate is 2.50%. Euro-area inflation was 3.8% in September, driven by energy inflation of 18.8%. Markets expect an ECB hike on 16-17 December. The DAX fell about 1.35% and led Europe lower; it had already closed down 1.26% at 25,017.81 on Tuesday. The ECB publishes its October meeting account on Thursday.
  • Japan. The Nikkei fell 2.79% to 57,011.62. USD/JPY is near 159.30, close to the 160 area that markets see as an intervention risk. The BoJ policy rate is 1.25%, and markets are weighing a hike to 1.50% on 18 December. Tokyo CPI is due early on Friday (Tokyo time).
  • China. The Shanghai Composite fell 1.77% to 3,476.47. The proposed US tariffs and the 10 January truce deadline are the main risks.
  • Oil. Brent fell about 4.4% to $87.37 in the European morning. The IEA sees a Q4 surplus, and Libya's Sharara pipeline has resumed. WTI was later up about 1.2% at $83.03 after reports of an attack on a tanker in the Strait of Hormuz. OPEC+ meets on Sunday 29 November to set 2027 output policy.
  • Australia. Inflation of 4.9% weighed on the ASX 200 (-0.39%).

Gold

The move. Gold had a wild session. Hot inflation data and the near-certain December Fed hike pushed it down to $3,426.40, but it recovered through the US afternoon and closed at $3,534.70, down only 0.71% on the day. That is a swing of more than $100 from the low, and a sign that buyers are active below $3,450.

The drivers. The headwinds are clear: markets price a 25 bp Fed hike on 9 December at about 94-98% and a further hike in January at about 63%, and real yields are high. Two things helped the afternoon rebound. Treasury yields fell, with the 10-year down 6 bp to 4.85% as investors looked for safety, and the dollar index slipped back to 103.71 after holding above 104 in the morning. The morning drop came alongside the equity sell-off, which points to forced selling across assets rather than a change in gold's own story. Central-bank buying remains the long-term support, but momentum players are leaving: BCA Research closed its four-year gold long after the break below $3,700, and gold is still about 13% below its level a month ago (about $4,006).

Volatility. High. The VIX closed at 28.29 after trading above 33, and gold's intraday range was roughly 3%.

Levels. Analysts point to support at $3,440-3,460 and then $3,380-3,400, with resistance at $3,583-3,600. Gold closed in the middle of that band.

Coming up. Thin holiday trading on Thursday and Friday can exaggerate moves. Tokyo CPI (Thursday 23:30 UK) and Bank of Japan Governor Ueda (Monday 01:05 UK) matter through the yen and the dollar. The Fed is in its quiet period before the 9 December decision.

What would change the picture. More positive: a daily close above $3,600, a softer dollar (for example a firmer yen after a hawkish Ueda), or the talk of a 50 bp Fed hike fading. More negative: a close below $3,440 and then $3,380, a 50 bp hike becoming the base case, or another round of cross-asset selling.

Silver

The move. Silver had a much worse day than gold. It touched $39.34, its lowest in 14 months, and traded around $39.7-40.3 in the US morning. It is down about 26% in a month, about 44% since the start of the year and more than 60% below its January peak of $121.58.

The drivers. The same Fed and dollar pressure as gold, without gold's central-bank support. Silver also tends to fall harder when risk appetite is weak.

Volatility. Very high: silver fell about 11% in a week.

Levels. Support is near $39.30, today's low area. The 14-day RSI is about 26, which is oversold, so a relief bounce is possible.

Coming up. The same events as gold, plus any news on industrial demand from China (official PMIs on Monday).

What would change the picture. More positive: an oversold bounce that holds above $40 and a recovery towards the $42.5 area where it traded on Tuesday. More negative: a clean break below $39.30, which would open a new leg lower.

Bitcoin and crypto

The move. Crypto ended a weak month with a small, fragile rebound. Bitcoin traded near $57,900 during the day and was about $60,100 in the US evening, up about 0.6%; Ethereum was near $1,685 and Solana gained about 1.2%. Short liquidations fuelled the bounce. Bitcoin is still about 24.5% below its 2 November level of $76,700 and far below its January high of $97,860.

The drivers. Money is leaving: US spot bitcoin funds have seen nearly $5 billion of outflows in November, their worst month since they launched in 2024. Leveraged positions have been flushed out, sentiment is at "extreme fear" (Fear & Greed index 13), and higher US rates are unhelpful for assets without a yield. One structural positive: the CFTC confirmed that regulated derivatives exchanges and clearing houses can count Bitcoin and Ethereum towards certain financial resource requirements.

Volatility. High, and holiday liquidity will make it worse until Monday.

Levels. Bitcoin: the $58,000-58,500 zone, where buyers stepped in during June, is the main support; on-chain data show about 400,000 BTC bought near $59,324, so holders there sit at break-even. A sustained close below $56,800 would thin out support, and analysts then see risk towards $46,700. Resistance is $64,600 (the 23 November high) and $66,532 (the 20-day EMA). Ethereum: support $1,580, resistance $1,870. Solana: support $68.75, resistance $85.65.

Coming up. Daily fund-flow figures, thin weekend trading and the 9 December Fed decision.

What would change the picture. More positive: a daily close above $64,600 and the fund outflows turning into inflows. More negative: a daily close below $56,800, which would put the $46,700 area in play.

US indices (S&P 500, Nasdaq 100, Dow)

The move. A sharp morning sell-off turned into one of the stronger recoveries of the month. The S&P 500 traded below 7,000 in the morning but closed only 0.25% lower at 7,182.78. The Nasdaq 100 was down 4.4% at 15:30 UK and closed 0.64% lower at 26,046.90. The Dow rose 0.33% to 50,182.95, back above 50,000, and the small-cap Russell 2000 gained 0.71%.

The drivers. Under the surface the market rotated rather than collapsed: materials (+2.07%), real estate (+0.92%), communication services (+0.89%), industrials (+0.83%) and financials (+0.71%) all rose, while technology (-0.83%) and health care (-0.56%) lagged. Most S&P 500 stocks rose. Falling Treasury yields helped, and credit markets were calm by the close (investment-grade and high-yield bond funds both rose). Against that sit a Fed that is about to hike, a new tariff threat and doubts about AI spending.

Volatility. The VIX closed at 28.29, up 7.32%, after trading above 33. Large daily swings are likely to continue.

Levels. The S&P 500 held 7,000 on a closing basis after breaking it during the day; the Dow is back above 50,000. At its low the Nasdaq 100 was close to 20% below its peak, the usual definition of a bear market.

Coming up. US markets are closed on Thursday and close early on Friday (18:00 UK), with the October goods trade balance on Friday (13:30 UK). Next week brings Nvidia's chief executive before Congress, and tariff details and the 10 January US-China deadline remain open.

What would change the picture. More positive: the broadening seen today continuing, with the Nasdaq 100 holding above today's lows. More negative: the S&P 500 closing back below 7,000, the Nasdaq 100 confirming a bear market, or a 50 bp hike.

Tech and chip funds (XLK, VGT, SMH)

The move. The chip sell-off eased into the close but did not reverse. Nvidia fell as much as about 8.7% during the day and closed 3.91% lower; AMD was down about 9.6% at midday, and Lam Research and Micron fell more than 10% intraday. The VanEck semiconductor fund (SMH) closed 1.01% lower and the iShares semiconductor fund 0.50% lower, well off their lows. The technology sector, which XLK and VGT track, fell 0.83%.

The drivers. The market is questioning the return on AI spending and how it is financed. S&P Global put Nvidia's AA rating on CreditWatch Negative, citing management and governance risk, and credit default swap spreads at SoftBank and CoreWeave have widened. Nvidia is down 27% in November even after its chief executive's return on Monday. Higher rates weigh most on the high valuations of tech.

Volatility. Very high for chips; lower for the broad tech funds, which also hold Apple and Microsoft.

Levels. No reliable fund-level levels were published today; the key reference is today's intraday low in the chip names.

Coming up. Nvidia's chief executive appears before Congress next week; any rating action by S&P Global; AI companies' financing news.

What would change the picture. More positive: Nvidia stabilising after the credit watch and the chip funds holding above today's lows. More negative: a downgrade, more AI-financing stress or a break of today's lows.

The ten US stocks

The moves. Meta rose 3.4% to about $610, bucking the AI sell-off. Amazon fell 3.12% (about $244) and Alphabet 2.13% (about $273), Microsoft fell about 1.2% (about $490) and Apple closed near $356 after trading as low as about $349. The defensives held up: Berkshire Hathaway rose 0.37% to $579.32 and JPMorgan closed almost unchanged at $316.74 (-0.11%). Visa was up about 0.9% at midday and closed near $424; Johnson & Johnson eased slightly (near $274); Walmart was little changed during the morning sell-off (about -0.1%) and closed near $132.

The drivers. Money rotated from the AI-heavy mega-caps into value, financials and defensives. Company news: a ransomware group reportedly leaked customer data from Visa, Mastercard and JPMorgan; JPMorgan recently raised its target for Johnson & Johnson to $300 (Neutral rating). Tariffs are a cost risk for importers and global supply chains such as Apple, Amazon and Walmart.

Levels. Berkshire Hathaway trades above its 50-day ($539.32) and 200-day ($503.61) averages, close to its one-year high of $596.92. Johnson & Johnson is about 5% below its 52-week high. JPMorgan's average analyst target is $373.65.

Coming up. Thanksgiving and Black Friday trading (a read on the consumer for Walmart and Amazon), tariff details and the 9 December Fed decision.

What would change the picture. More positive for the mega-caps: AI financing worries easing and a calmer Nvidia. More negative: further AI-capex doubts, tariff escalation hitting Apple and Amazon, or higher yields; the defensives (Berkshire, Johnson & Johnson, Walmart) would likely keep outperforming in that case.
