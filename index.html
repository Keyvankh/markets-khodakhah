Afternoon update

Written 15:05 UK, after the US open. Information only: not advice, and not used by K1's trading.

  • The overnight surge has mostly faded. Nasdaq 100 futures were up about 2.3% before the bell, but the index opened only 0.66% higher (26,219 at 14:42 UK) and was 26,209 (+0.6%) at 15:03 UK. Nvidia reversed a premarket gain to trade 1.9% lower at $146.39, and the chip fund SMH slipped back to $424 after touching $435.55. Amazon (+2.0%), Meta (+2.4%) and JPMorgan (+1.7%) led K1's ten stocks; Alphabet was 0.7% lower.
  • A wider trade gap, little else from the US. The October advance goods deficit was $149.5 billion against a $133.0 billion forecast and $135.9 billion previously (exports $215.8 billion, imports $365.3 billion), a fourth straight widening. Wholesale inventories rose 0.9% (previous 0.8%). No Fed, Bank of England or ECB speakers were scheduled. CME FedWatch last showed a 97.4% chance of a 25 bp Fed hike on 9 December and about a 63% chance of 50 bp in total by January.
  • Yields and the dollar edged up. The US 10-year yield was 4.89% (+3 bp) and the dollar index 103.97 (+0.2%) at 15:03 UK, which took some shine off the metals.
  • Gold kept most of its jump, silver did better. Spot gold reached $3,685 and was $3,635 (+1.9%) at 15:03 UK; silver was $43.56 (+4.9%). Reports cite a pause in the dollar's rally after a 22-month high and better physical demand in India and China.
  • Bitcoin gave back the morning bounce. The Deribit expiry settled near $61,600 at 08:00 UK without a jump, but Bitcoin then slid to a low of $58,791 and was $58,997 (-2.5% since midnight UTC) at 15:03 UK, back inside the bottom of its range zone; Ethereum fell 2.9% to $1,639. No single trigger was reported.
  • What to watch. US stocks close at 18:00 UK and bonds at 19:00 UK, so late moves may be thin. Then OPEC+ on Sunday (Brent fell 1.7% to $94.89 at 15:03 UK after touching $98.06), January Brent's expiry and China's PMI on Monday, ISM and JOLTS on Tuesday and US jobs on 4 December.

Today in brief

  • Risk appetite returned overnight. US index futures rose sharply ahead of today's shortened Wall Street session: at about 07:15 UK, E-mini Nasdaq 100 futures were up 2.5% at 26,760, S&P 500 futures up 1.7% at 7,315.5 and Dow futures up 0.9% at 50,664. Renewed buying of AI and chip stocks after Wednesday's dip is the main reason given.
  • Asia rallied hard. The Kospi rose 4.3%, the Shanghai Composite 3.7% and the Nikkei 225 2.1% to 58,711, helped by chip buying and a pledge from China's Cabinet to strengthen the "national team" and plan new market-support measures.
  • Gold and silver bounced strongly. Spot gold rose to $3,660 at about 07:15 UK (+2.6%, high $3,667) after closing Thursday at $3,566. Silver jumped 6.6% to $44.27. Reports point to value buying after 14-month lows, thin holiday trade and a slightly softer dollar.
  • Bitcoin climbed back above $61,000 before a large options expiry. Bitcoin was $61,795 at about 07:15 UK (+2.1% since midnight UTC). About $6.8 billion of Deribit bitcoin options expire at 08:00 UTC (08:00 UK) today.
  • Oil stays near a one-month high. Brent traded around $96.6-97.3 overnight after Thursday's settlement, the highest since 6 November, as a strike on Saudi Aramco's Jazan refinery and Hormuz tanker attacks kept supply risk alive. OPEC+ meets on Sunday.
  • Japan's inflation was softer than expected. Tokyo CPI rose 2.8% y/y in November against a 3.0% forecast, which eases pressure on the Bank of Japan a little; USD/JPY held at 159.3.
  • Today: a thin, short session. French and euro-area data this morning, the US advance goods trade balance at 13:30 UK, and an early 18:00 UK close for US stocks. Month-end and low volume can exaggerate moves.

United States

No data on Thursday. Markets were shut for Thanksgiving. Wednesday's releases (actual / forecast / previous) still set the tone: core PCE prices for October 3.3% y/y / 3.2% / 3.1%, the highest since late 2023; Q3 GDP second estimate 4.3% annualised / 3.5% / 3.4%; initial jobless claims 189,000 / 200,000 / 203,000. Growth is strong and inflation is moving the wrong way.

The Fed. The funds rate is 3.75-4.00%. CME FedWatch showed a 97.8% chance of a 25 bp hike on 9 December on Wednesday morning, with nothing priced for 50 bp. Officials are in their blackout, so data now leads: ISM manufacturing and JOLTS on Tuesday, then the jobs figures.

Markets. Wednesday ended with the S&P 500 at 7,182.78 (-0.25%), the Nasdaq 100 at 26,046.90 (-0.64%) and the Dow at 50,182.95 (+0.33%), after intraday falls of 3.2% in the S&P 500 and 4.6% in the Nasdaq Composite. Nvidia fell 3.9% to $149.30 after S&P Global warned of a possible rating cut over governance; Meta (+3.4%) and Visa (+1.8%) gained. TipRanks says the main indices still go into today up about 4% on the week, a rally that began when Jensen Huang returned as Nvidia's chief executive on Monday.

This morning. At about 07:15 UK, S&P 500 futures were 1.7% higher, Nasdaq 100 futures 2.5% and Dow futures 0.9%, which Trading Economics ties to the AI trade regaining strength. Treasury yields rose in early trade (10-year 4.91%, 2-year 4.68%), so bonds are not sharing the relief. US stocks close at 18:00 UK. The only data is the October advance goods trade balance at 13:30 UK (forecast -$133.0 billion, previous -$134.9 billion); Black Friday spending reports follow over the weekend. The proposed US tariffs on 14 partners will not stack on July's levies, exclude technology and are unlikely before February or March; China has promised a response.

United Kingdom

There was no major UK data on Thursday and none is due today. Bank Rate is 4.00% after the 5 November hike, and market reports put the chance of another 25 bp rise on 17 December at almost 80%, with a further move in February also leaned on. Governor Bailey has said further rises are "more likely than not" if energy prices stay high.

The 10-year gilt yield was about 5.16% this morning. Sterling was $1.3123 at about 07:15 UK, slightly firmer overnight but down more than 2% in November, with a recent one-year low near $1.3011. The FTSE 100 rose 1.75% on Thursday to 10,609.29 as the milder US tariff plan lifted all of Europe. With Brent near $97 and sterling soft, imported inflation remains the Bank's main worry. Mortgage approvals (previous 53,443) and consumer credit are due on Monday at 09:30 UK.

Europe and the world

Euro area. Europe had its best day in weeks on Thursday: the Euro Stoxx 50 rose 2.1% to 6,019, the CAC 40 1.6% to 8,109 and the DAX 2.1% to 25,216, on the milder US tariff plan and a recovery in chip stocks. The German 10-year yield was about 3.29% this morning. Markets expect an ECB hike in December. Today brings French flash CPI at 07:45 UK (forecast 3.2% y/y, previous 3.4%), German unemployment at 08:55 UK (rate forecast 6.4%) and euro-area economic sentiment at 10:00 UK (forecast 97.2, previous 96.9); S&P reviews France's rating tonight. Tuesday's flash HICP (forecast 4.0%) is the week's key European number.

Japan. Tokyo CPI rose 2.8% y/y in November against a 3.0% forecast (previous 2.8%); excluding fresh food 2.9% (forecast 3.0%, previous 2.6%); excluding food and energy 2.5% (forecast 2.7%, previous 2.4%). The softer print slightly cools talk of faster Bank of Japan hikes. USD/JPY held at 159.3, near the area where Japan has intervened before. The Nikkei rose 2.1% to 58,711, back above 58,000, led by chip and heavy-industry stocks.

China and Asia. China's Cabinet pledged to plan new market-support measures and strengthen the "national team"; the Shanghai Composite rose 3.7% to 3,606 and the Hang Seng 1.9% to 22,656. The Kospi jumped 4.3% to 4,916, recovering from Wednesday's 6% fall. Toyota's October global sales fell 6.3% y/y, a ninth monthly decline.

Oil and geopolitics. Brent traded around $96.2-97.3 and WTI around $85.4-86.3 in Asian hours, heading for oil's first weekly gain after a run of losses. A projectile hit Saudi Aramco's Jazan refinery on Thursday, halting some operations, after Houthi claims of attacks on Ras Tanura, Jeddah and Jazan; Iran's Revolutionary Guards said two tankers hit mines near Khasab in the Strait of Hormuz. ANZ says Brent could push above $100. OPEC+ meets on Sunday and is expected to hold output targets for at least the first quarter of 2027.

Gold

Gold closed Thursday at $3,566.30 on the TradingView daily chart (-0.65%), after a quiet $3,551-3,607 range with New York shut. Overnight it jumped to $3,660 at about 07:15 UK (+2.6%, high $3,667), almost 7% above Wednesday's low of $3,427, the lowest since late August 2025. There was no single trigger: reports cite bargain buying, thin holiday trade, a dollar index easing to 103.69 and the broad return of risk appetite in Asia; Indian demand was visible on MCX.

The drivers underneath have barely changed. A Fed hike on 9 December is 97.8% priced, the ECB and the Bank of England are likely to follow, and US core inflation is 3.3%, so real yields stay high and the cost of holding a metal with no yield stays high too. The US 10-year yield rose to 4.91% this morning, a headwind the rebound is ignoring for now. UBS points to structural support from central-bank reserve diversification, Chinese buying and resilient ETF holdings, but expects a range-bound market near term. Gulf attacks and $97 oil add some haven and inflation-hedge interest, though rates and the dollar have dominated recently.

Volatility is high: $100-200 daily ranges have been common this month, and a 2.6% jump in a thin Asian session can reverse once London and New York trade in full.

Levels: price sits in the middle of the Swing Intelligence discount zone ($3,554-3,744). Resistance at $3,651-3,667 (the 23 November close and this morning's high), then $3,700, $3,744 (top of the zone) and $3,856. Support at $3,554-3,566 (bottom of the zone and Thursday's close), then about $3,515 and $3,427.

Events: Tuesday's ISM and JOLTS, US jobs data, the Fed on 9 December and the Bank of England on 17 December.

Triggers. More positive: a daily close above $3,667 that holds into next week, then a move through $3,744, especially with the dollar index below 103 and yields falling. More negative: a fall back below $3,554, which would mark this as a holiday spike, a close below $3,427, or data that revive talk of a 50 bp hike.

Silver

Silver closed Thursday at $41.54 (+0.33%) and jumped 6.6% overnight to $44.27 at about 07:15 UK (high $44.60), 16% above Wednesday's $38.20 low but still about 38% below its late-August high near $71. As the more beaten-down, higher-beta metal it gained more than gold, helped on the industrial side by the rally in Asian chip stocks and China's support pledge. The gold-to-silver ratio fell to about 83 from about 86.

Silver faces gold's rate and dollar headwinds, but also trades on manufacturing and AI-hardware demand, which looked brighter overnight; the tariff exemption for technology helps too. Volatility is extreme, with 4-7% daily swings now normal.

Levels: price is in the upper half of the Swing Intelligence discount zone ($41.50-46.44). Resistance at $44.3-44.6, then $46.4 and $47.4. Support at $43.2, $41.5, $40.7 and $38.20.

Events: China's NBS PMI on Monday (forecast 49.4, previous 49.3), the RatingDog PMI on Tuesday (forecast 51.5) and US ISM manufacturing (forecast 55.2, previous 55.5).

Triggers. More positive: a daily close above $44.6 and then $46.4, with firmer Chinese PMIs. More negative: a close back below $41.5, and especially $40.7, or a new sell-off in chip stocks.

Bitcoin and crypto

Bitcoin closed Thursday's daily candle at $60,577 on Binance (+0.2%) and rose to $61,795 at about 07:15 UK (+2.1% since midnight UTC), about 29% below its September high near $87,400. Altcoins did a little better: Ethereum $1,735 (+2.8%), Solana $72.98 (+2.9%), BNB $566 (+2.3%), Chainlink $9.59 (+3.4%) and Hedera $0.0558 (+3.5%).

The bounce is short covering and better risk mood rather than new demand: about $66 million was liquidated in 24 hours, roughly 71% shorts. US spot bitcoin funds lost a record $5.39 billion in November up to Wednesday ($453 million that day) and were shut on Thursday. 21Shares notes stablecoin supply has fallen $10.7 billion in 18 days, the most since 2022, a sign of cash leaving crypto. The Fear and Greed index is 12 ("extreme fear"), and higher rate expectations remain the macro headwind.

Volatility: about $6.8 billion of Deribit bitcoin options expire at 08:00 UK. The largest position, at the $65,000 strike, is mostly puts, and The Daily Digits says the market "is set up for defense, not for a squeeze". Swings around expiry are likely.

Levels: price is near the top of the Swing Intelligence discount zone ($58,357-63,196). Resistance at $62,000, $63,200, the 20-day average near $65,300 and $69,650; the 200-day average near $72,000 marks the longer trend. Support at $60,000, $58,357-58,500 (bottom of the zone and June's closing low), $55,130 and the realised price near $53,100.

Events: the expiry, today's ETF flows, Tuesday's ISM and JOLTS, the Fed on 9 December.

Triggers. More positive: a daily close above $63,200 and then $65,300, with net ETF inflows and stable stablecoin supply. More negative: a close below $58,500, renewed heavy ETF outflows or yields rising further.

Tech and chip funds (XLK, VGT, SMH)

On Wednesday XLK closed at $155.79 (-0.8%), VGT at $102.56 (-0.7%) and SMH at $422.46 (-1.0%), well off their lows (SMH touched $393.88). Nasdaq futures up 2.5% imply a strong rebound today, after Asian chipmakers led the Nikkei and the Kospi higher and with technology excluded from the US tariff plan. The risks are specific: S&P Global's warning on Nvidia's rating, its leadership turmoil, worries about slower AI investment and the debt behind it, and the Bank of England's warning about stretched AI valuations. Higher yields weigh most on these long-duration stocks. SMH swung about 7% from low to close on Wednesday, so large gaps are normal.

Levels: SMH resistance at Wednesday's high of $423, support at $394; XLK support at $148.4 and VGT at $97.8 (Wednesday's lows).

Triggers. More positive: SMH holding above $423 into next week and Nvidia stabilising. More negative: an actual Nvidia downgrade, new credit stress at AI borrowers, or SMH closing below $394.

About the range zones

The coloured zones come from Swing Intelligence, a TradingView indicator by Keyvan Khodakhah. They split the recent trading range into a premium area near the top, an equilibrium band in the middle and a discount area near the bottom, so you can see at a glance where price sits.

They are context for the analysis, not buy or sell signals. The indicator is free on TradingView.
