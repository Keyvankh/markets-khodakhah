Tariff relief lifts Europe while gold, silver and Bitcoin hold near their lows

A milder-than-feared US tariff plan sent the DAX up 2.3% with Wall Street shut for Thanksgiving, but near-certain December rate hikes keep pressure on metals and crypto.

Today in brief

  • Relief over US tariffs lifted Europe. The new US tariff package proved milder than feared: the duties will not stack on July's 10-12.5% levies, exclude technology and Section 232 goods, and are unlikely to take effect before February or March. The DAX closed about 2.3% higher at 25,271 and the FTSE 100 about 1.8% higher at 10,612.
  • Wall Street was shut for Thanksgiving. US stock and bond markets were closed and reopen for a shortened session on Friday (stocks close 18:00 UK). Wednesday's closes stand: S&P 500 7,182.78 (-0.25%), Nasdaq 100 26,046.90 (-0.64%), Dow 50,182.95 (+0.33%), after an intraday fall of more than 4% in the Nasdaq.
  • Three central banks lean towards December hikes. Fed funds futures price about 96% for a 25 bp Fed hike on 9 December; ECB accounts showed many members could have backed a hike in October, and futures price about 90% for December; markets give the Bank of England almost 80% for 17 December.
  • Gold held above $3,550 but could not regain $3,600. Spot gold traded between $3,551 and $3,607 and stood at $3,570 at 16:30 UK (-0.5%), a day after its lowest level since late August 2025.
  • Crypto stayed pinned near its lows. Bitcoin traded around $60,370 at 16:30 UK. US spot bitcoin funds have lost a record $5.39 billion in November, with a seventh straight day of outflows on Wednesday ($453 million).
  • Oil rose for a third day. January Brent reached $95.58 at 13:08 UK on Middle East supply worries, ahead of Sunday's OPEC+ meeting.
  • Thin liquidity into month-end. Friday is a half-day in New York and Monday is November's last full session, so moves can be exaggerated.

United States

No new data today. Thanksgiving closed stock and bond markets. The week's key figures came on Wednesday (actual / forecast / previous):

  • Core PCE prices (October): +0.3% m/m / +0.3% / +0.3%; 3.3% y/y / 3.2% / 3.1%, the highest since late 2023. Headline PCE 3.8% y/y.
  • Q3 GDP, second estimate: 4.3% annualised / 3.5% / 3.4% advance.
  • Initial jobless claims (week to 21 November): 189,000 / 200,000 / 203,000.

The Fed. The funds rate is 3.75-4.00% after a hike in September. Market reports put the chance of another 25 bp hike on 9 December at about 96%. The debate has moved from "whether" to "how many": pricing now points to two more hikes in total rather than three, and talk of a 50 bp move in December has faded. Officials are in their pre-meeting blackout.

Markets. Wednesday's session was a round trip: the S&P 500 fell as much as 3.2% and the Nasdaq Composite as much as 4.6% on hot inflation, the tariff threat and the AI sell-off, before buyers returned. Nvidia still lost 3.9% to $149.30 after S&P Global warned it could cut the company's credit rating over governance concerns, days after Jensen Huang's return as chief executive. Micron (-4.5%), Lam Research (-5.7%) and Oracle (-5.1%) also fell, while Meta rose 3.4% and HP 11.5%. Treasuries rallied, with the 10-year yield down about 6 bp to 4.86%. S&P 500 futures were about 0.5% higher early on Thursday, helped by the tariff news.

Trade. The US proposal targets 14 trading partners, including China, Japan and the European Union, on the grounds of "structural excess industrial capacity". The new duties will not stack on July's 10-12.5% tariffs, exclude technology and goods already under Section 232, and have a hearing set for 25 January, so they are unlikely to start before February or March. China said it would hit back.

United Kingdom

There was no major UK data. Bank Rate is 4.00% after November's hike. On Wednesday, Governor Bailey, Deputy Governor Lombardelli and MPC member Mann said persistently high energy prices are an inflation threat that "cannot be ignored", and Megan Greene said further tightening would be appropriate unless the Middle East conflict ends or the data improve. Bloomberg reports almost 80% odds of a 25 bp hike on 17 December; Scotiabank puts swap pricing at about 19 bp.

The Bank published its Financial Policy Committee record and Financial Stability Report today. Market reports say it will cut capital requirements for the largest UK-focused lenders by 60 bp, held the countercyclical buffer at 2%, and flagged stretched AI valuations and a large AI-related debt wave. Deputy Governor Breeden said the Bank needs to "push hard" on the resilience of the gilt repo market. Gilts lagged Bunds: the 10-year yield edged up to about 5.16%. Sterling held near $1.311, close to its weakest since April 2025. The FTSE 100 rose 1.8%, led by Imperial Brands (+16% on reports that Altria may bid) and Babcock (+6.7% after an £8 billion government contract).

Europe and the world

Euro area. The accounts of the ECB's 28-29 October meeting showed that a large number of members could have supported a hike then, and that a hike would "simply frontload" tightening that is needed; staff said rates still lie within the range of neutral estimates. Futures price about a 90% chance of a 25 bp hike at the December meeting. Data: German GfK consumer climate (December) -30.2 / -29.9 / -30.9, still close to record-low territory; euro-area M3 growth 3.6% / 3.5% / 3.5%; loans to companies 4.5% (previous 4.2%). Pablo Hernandez de Cos has majority support to succeed Christine Lagarde, who steps down on 7 February 2027. Stocks rallied hard: the STOXX 600 rose about 1.7% from near two-month lows, with chipmakers Infineon (+4.5%) and Soitec (+7.5%) among the leaders. S&P reviews France's rating on Friday.

Asia. The Nikkei rose 0.9% to 57,499 as dip buyers returned to AI names (SoftBank +7.3%, Kioxia +12.6%). The Kospi added 0.3% after a 6.2% fall on Wednesday; the Hang Seng gained 1.2%. Bank of Japan board member Tamura called for faster rate hikes; USD/JPY held around 159.3, close to the zone where Japan has intervened before. Tokyo CPI is due tonight.

Oil and geopolitics. January Brent rose for a third day to $95.58 at 13:08 UK (range $93.48-96.20), after settling at $94.61 (+3.5%) on Wednesday; WTI was $84.70. Houthi forces claimed an attack on Saudi Arabia's Ras Tanura refinery, CNN reported loadings there were halted, and Iran's Revolutionary Guards stopped a container ship near the Strait of Hormuz. Oil is still about 19% below its 30 October close, with the IEA expecting a fourth-quarter surplus. OPEC+ meets on Sunday 29 November and is expected to roll over output targets for at least the first quarter of 2027. January Brent expires on Monday.

Gold

Gold spent the day in a narrow range ($3,551-3,607) and stood at $3,570 at 16:30 UK, down 0.5%. It is consolidating after Wednesday's flush to $3,427, its lowest since late August 2025, and is roughly 24% below its peak near $4,697 on the TradingView daily chart.

The drivers remain hostile. Three central banks are close to hiking in December, which raises the opportunity cost of holding a metal that pays no yield. Real yields are high: the US 10-year nominal yield is 4.86% with core inflation at 3.3%, and the dollar index is near 104 after a 20-month high on Tuesday. Wednesday's fall in Treasury yields gave gold a little relief, but not enough to change the picture. Safe-haven demand has not offset this, even with fresh Gulf tensions, which says the market is treating rates and the dollar as the dominant forces. Indian buyers stepped in on the dip (MCX gold rose about 2%), a sign of physical demand at lower prices.

Volatility is high: daily ranges of $100-200 have been common this month, and the market is thin with New York closed.

Levels: resistance first at $3,600-3,607 (today's high), then $3,745 (top of the Swing Intelligence discount zone) and $3,856. Support is at $3,554 (bottom of that zone), the round $3,500 that analysts at LKP Securities call strong support, and Wednesday's low of $3,427. Price sits at the very bottom of the discount zone.

Coming events: US ISM manufacturing and JOLTS on Tuesday, then US jobs data next week before the Fed on 9 December; euro-area flash inflation on Tuesday.

Triggers. More positive: a daily close back above $3,600-3,745, a softer dollar (DXY back below 103), or Treasury yields falling further on weak data. More negative: a daily close below $3,500, and especially below $3,427, or a hawkish surprise (talk of 50 bp, or of more hikes in 2027).

Silver

Silver held at $41.54 at 16:30 UK (+0.35%), after a 14-month low at $38.20 on Wednesday. It has fallen about 42% from its late-August high near $71.

Silver is suffering twice: as a precious metal it faces the same rates and dollar headwinds as gold, and as an industrial metal it is hurt by the tech and AI sell-off and by fears that tariffs slow global manufacturing. Today's tariff relief and firmer copper ($11,826-12,093 a tonne on the LME) helped a little. The gold-to-silver ratio is about 86, high by recent standards, which shows silver remains the weaker of the two.

Volatility is extreme: daily swings of 4-5% are common.

Levels: resistance at $43.2-44.3 (Monday's close and Tuesday's high), then $46.4 (top of the discount zone). Support at $41.5 (bottom of the discount zone, where price sits now), then $40.7 and Wednesday's low of $38.20.

Coming events: China's official PMIs on Monday and the RatingDog manufacturing PMI on Tuesday (forecast 51.5), US ISM manufacturing on Tuesday (forecast 55.2, previous 55.5).

Triggers. More positive: a daily close above $44.3 together with stronger Chinese PMIs. More negative: a close below $40.7 and then $38.20, or further heavy selling in chip and AI stocks.

Bitcoin and crypto

Bitcoin traded around $60,370 at 16:30 UK, roughly flat on the day after a range of $59,863-61,484. It is about 31% below its September high near $87,400 and only just above the $55,130 low of 22 November. Ethereum was $1,684, Solana $70.6, BNB $550, Chainlink $9.16 (+1.3%) and Hedera $0.0535 (+2.8%).

Fund flows remain the main headwind: US spot bitcoin funds have lost $5.39 billion this month, a record above June's $4.51 billion, including $453 million on Wednesday and $3.05 billion over the past seven sessions. BlackRock's IBIT alone has lost about $4.5 billion in November; only Morgan Stanley's and WisdomTree's funds took in money. Higher rate expectations and the AI rout are the reasons analysts give, and some argue that inflows need long-term Treasury yields to stabilise. Derivatives show some short covering: $264 million of positions were liquidated in 24 hours (more shorts than longs) and open interest rose 3.4% to $40.1 billion. Galaxy Digital has withdrawn its call that the bear market had bottomed.

Volatility is high and liquidity is thin over the US holiday, with a large monthly options expiry on Friday.

Levels: resistance at $63,200 (top of the Swing Intelligence discount zone), $65,000-65,350 (the 20-day average) and $69,650. Support at $58,357-58,525 (bottom of the zone and the June closing low Galaxy watches), then $55,130.

Coming events: Friday's options expiry, US ISM on Tuesday, then ETF flow data each day; the Fed on 9 December.

Triggers. More positive: a daily close above $65,350 and the first days of net ETF inflows. More negative: a daily close below $58,500, which Galaxy says would confirm a new low, and continued daily outflows.

US indices (S&P 500, Nasdaq 100, Dow)

US markets were closed today. On Wednesday the S&P 500 closed at 7,182.78 (-0.25%), the Nasdaq 100 at 26,046.90 (-0.64%) and the Dow at 50,182.95 (+0.33%); SPY closed at $716.70, QQQ at $633.93 and DIA at $501.27. The rebound from the morning lows was large and broad.

The drivers are mixed. Against: core inflation at 3.3%, near-certain Fed hikes, a VIX at 28 and doubts about AI spending, governance at Nvidia and credit stress at AI-linked borrowers. For: strong growth (Q3 GDP 4.3%), falling Treasury yields on Wednesday, today's tariff relief and futures that were 0.5-0.8% higher before the holiday. The Dow's value and defensive names are holding up far better than the Nasdaq.

Volatility: the VIX closed at 28.29 after touching nearly 35; intraday ranges of 3-4% in the Nasdaq show how fragile sentiment is.

Levels (QQQ): resistance at $645-651 (top of the discount zone and Monday's close), then $677-692 (the equilibrium zone). Support at $622 (bottom of the discount zone) and $606-608 (the lows of 20 and 25 November). The S&P 500's intraday low on Wednesday was 6,970.

Coming events: Friday's half-day session, Monday's month-end, ISM manufacturing and JOLTS on Tuesday.

Triggers. More positive: QQQ closing above $651 with the VIX back below 22. More negative: a break below $606, or a VIX close above 35.

Tech and chip funds (XLK, VGT, SMH)

On Wednesday XLK closed at $155.79 (-0.8%), VGT at $102.56 (-0.7%) and SMH at $422.46 (-1.0%), all well off their lows (SMH traded as low as $393.88). These funds carry the AI risk most directly. Nvidia fell 3.9%; memory and equipment makers fell harder (Micron -4.5%, Lam Research -5.7%, SanDisk -5.6%). Outside the US the mood improved today: SK Hynix, Kioxia, Infineon and Soitec all rose, and Japan's AI names led Tokyo higher.

The tariff exclusions for technology are a clear relief for these funds. Against that, the Bank of England today joined those warning about stretched AI valuations and AI-linked debt.

Volatility: SMH swung about 7% from low to close on Wednesday.

Levels: SMH support at $394 (Wednesday's low); XLK support at $148.4 and VGT at $97.8 (Wednesday's lows).

Coming events: Friday's half day, then ISM manufacturing on Tuesday.

Triggers. More positive: Nvidia stabilising after the rating warning, and SMH closing above Monday's level. More negative: an actual Nvidia downgrade or new credit stress at AI borrowers, and SMH closing below $394.

The ten US stocks

The split is clear: the AI spenders (Amazon, Alphabet, Microsoft) are punished on capital-spending and return worries, while Meta has bucked the trend for two days. Berkshire, Visa and JPMorgan held up as investors favour cash-rich, less AI-dependent names; higher rates support bank margins but tighter policy and credit stress are a risk. Johnson & Johnson and Walmart, normally defensive, slipped slightly. Walmart and Amazon also face Black Friday and Cyber Monday, a test of the US consumer.

Volatility: single-stock ranges were wide; Amazon and Alphabet both traded about 4% from their lows to their closes.

Levels: Wednesday's lows are the near-term markers: Amazon $235.96, Alphabet $262.73, Microsoft $477.39, Meta $567.75, Apple $345.13.

Coming events: holiday shopping figures over the weekend, ISM and JOLTS on Tuesday.

Triggers. More positive: strong holiday sales and calmer AI news. More negative: weak holiday spending, a break of Wednesday's lows, or a fresh tariff escalation from China.
